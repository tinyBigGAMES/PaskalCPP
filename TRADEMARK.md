Subject to applicable law, you may:

## Official versus modified distributions

If you distribute an unchanged official release, describe it accurately and do not imply that you created it. Preserve all applicable source-code licenses and notices.

If you distribute a modified fork:

- Use a distinct project name and visual identity for your distribution so users will not confuse it with the official PaskalCPP releases.
- Clearly describe the relationship to PaskalCPP, for example: "Based on PaskalCPP; independently modified and maintained by [project name]."
- Do not label your fork as an "official PaskalCPP" release or suggest endorsement by tinyBigGAMES™ LLC without authorization.
- Follow all applicable requirements of the MPL-2.0 and other relevant licenses.

The above branding guidance is separate from the MPL-2.0. It does not limit any permissions granted under the copyright license or rights permitted by applicable law.

## Uses requiring authorization where protected by law

Please obtain written permission before:

- Using PaskalCPP branding in a way that suggests your fork, product, service, or organization is an official or endorsed project.
- Using the official PaskalCPP logo as the primary branding for an independently maintained distribution.
- Registering confusingly similar project names, domains, or branding for competing software or services.
- Using PaskalCPP branding in merchandise or advertising in a way that implies a relationship with tinyBigGAMES™ LLC.

## No restrictions on your compiled applications

You do not need permission to use the PaskalCPP name in a truthful statement such as "Built with PaskalCPP." Applications you build with the language are not required to display the PaskalCPP logo or name solely because you used the toolchain.
